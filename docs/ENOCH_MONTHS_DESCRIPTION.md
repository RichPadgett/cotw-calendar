Here’s a synopsis of the alternate month-name icons and the symbolic ideas behind each one:

1. **Abib — “Green Ears of Grain”**

   * Depicted with fresh barley stalks.
   * Represents new life, first fruits, springtime, and agricultural renewal.
   * Strong connection to the biblical barley harvest and Exodus season.

2. **Ziv — “Radiance”**

   * Illustrated as a glowing oil lantern.
   * Symbolizes light, brilliance, spiritual illumination, and guidance.
   * The radiating glow reinforces the meaning of splendor or brightness.

3. **Sivan — “Binding”**

   * Depicted with a knotted rope before mountains.
   * Represents covenant, joining together, and commitment.
   * The mountain backdrop subtly evokes Sinai and covenantal unity.

4. **Tammuz — “Heat”**

   * Shown as an intense blazing sun over dry land.
   * Symbolizes scorching summer, testing, drought, and endurance.
   * The cracked earth emphasizes severity and refinement.

5. **Menachem Av — “Comfort of My Father”**

   * Depicted with ancient ruins illuminated by hopeful light.
   * Balances mourning and consolation.
   * The broken stones represent destruction, while the sunlight suggests restoration and comfort.

6. **Elul — “Search”**

   * Illustrated with a magnifying glass over a heart.
   * Represents introspection, repentance, and examining one’s inner life.
   * The heart emphasizes sincerity and spiritual preparation.

7. **Ethanim — “Strong”**

   * Depicted with a crown and shofar.
   * Symbolizes kingship, authority, power, and sacred proclamation.
   * The shofar connects to trumpet blasts and holy convocations.

8. **Bul — “Produce”**

   * Illustrated with a basket of fruit and harvest produce.
   * Represents abundance, provision, ingathering, and fertility.
   * The overflowing basket reinforces harvest completion.

9. **Kislev — “Confidence / Trust”**

   * Depicted as a fortified tower and shield.
   * Symbolizes protection, steadfastness, faith, and refuge.
   * The imagery conveys security during darker winter months.

10. **Tevet — “Good”**

* Illustrated with a bare winter tree in snow.
* Symbolizes endurance, hidden life, patience, and quiet goodness beneath hardship.
* The dormant tree suggests hope waiting beneath winter.

11. **Shevat — “Tribe”**

* Depicted as people surrounding a flourishing tree.
* Represents community, family roots, unity, and shared inheritance.
* The tree imagery also reflects growth and fruitfulness.

12. **Adar — “Mighty”**

* Illustrated with a royal crown and festive mask.
* Symbolizes triumph, joy, reversal, celebration, and boldness.
* The celebratory imagery reflects victory and gladness associated with Purim themes.
